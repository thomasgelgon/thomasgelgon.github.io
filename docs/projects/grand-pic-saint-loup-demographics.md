# Demographic Diagnosis — Grand Pic Saint-Loup Territorial Report

![Project overview image](../assets/images/project5-cover.png)

## Overview

Contributed demographic analysis (migration and aging maps and written sections) to a 100-page collective territorial diagnostic produced by my master's degree class for the Development Council (CODEV) of the Communauté de communes du Grand Pic Saint-Loup (Hérault), as part of a real commission from the local authority.

**Study Area:** Communauté de communes du Grand Pic Saint-Loup, Hérault
**Duration:** September 2025 – December 2025 (4 months)
**Role:** Contributor (group project — migration and aging sections only)
**Status:** Completed

---

## Methods & Tools

**Data Sources**

- INSEE census data (population, migration, household size)
- IGN geographic data
- Local planning and prospective documents (PLUi, SCoT, Projet de Territoire)
- Field surveys (questionnaires)

**Processing Steps**

1. Collection and analysis of INSEE demographic data (population growth, migratory/natural balance, aging index)
2. Field surveys (questionnaires) to complement statistical data with residents' perspectives
3. Cartography of demographic evolution and migration patterns by commune (2016–2022)
4. Cartography of the population aging index by commune
5. Writing of accompanying analytical text on demographic trends and territorial disparities

**Tools Used**

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Magrit | Thematic cartography of demographic and migration data |
| Excel | Processing and analysis of INSEE statistical data |

## Key Findings

- The territory's population grew from under 10,000 to 51,217 inhabitants between 1975 and 2022, driven by a migratory balance consistently exceeding the natural balance — largely fuelled by Montpellier's peri-urban expansion, attracting affluent families and retirees in search of a residential, nature-close setting
- Only 3 communes recorded a negative migratory balance between 2016–2022 (Mas-de-Londres, Assas, Ferrières-les-Verreries), showing the territory remains broadly attractive with no short/medium-term depopulation risk
- The aging index reveals a stark geographic contrast: communes closer to Montpellier tend to have older populations, while more remote communes skew younger
- Despite this overall attractiveness, the territory faces a key challenge in retaining its younger residents, who are pushed out by high housing costs, an oversized/unsuited housing stock, and a lack of professional opportunities in more remote areas such as the Buèges valley
