# More-than-Human Agency in Landscape Memory and Heritage

![Project overview image](../assets/images/project4-cover.png)

## Overview

Essay for a "Living Landscapes" course on cultural geography, examining how more-than-human agents — trees, fungi, wastelands — actively shape cultural memory, heritage and ecological transformation, and how they challenge dominant narratives of landscape, land use and value.

**Duration:** May 2025
**Role:** Solo project
**Status:** Completed

---

## Methods & Tools

**Data Sources**

- Key texts in more-than-human geography (Whatmore, Tsing, Descola)
- Academic literature on specific case studies (arborglyphs in Chycina, Poland; Ponary forest, Lithuania; post-Soviet wastelands; nuclear landscapes)
- Course lectures

**Processing Steps**

1. Critical review of key more-than-human geography literature
2. Selection and analysis of case studies illustrating more-than-human agency (treescapes, wastelands, nuclear landscapes)
3. Critical analysis of examples to address the essay question
4. Synthesis into a structured argument on landscape co-production and its challenge to dominant narratives

---

## Key Findings

- Arborglyphs carved into trees act as non-institutional memorials, preserving otherwise-lost histories (e.g. war-era carvings on beech bark in Chycina, Poland) and resisting erasure by time or dominant narratives
- Forests can act as ambiguous witnesses to historical violence, both concealing and preserving memory, as at the Ponary forest massacre site in Lithuania
- Mycorrhizal networks provide physical evidence of tree intelligence, communication and memory, reinforcing trees' status as active agents rather than passive scenery
- Wastelands and nuclear landscapes, though often dismissed as unproductive or dead, are shaped by more-than-human agents into living landscapes that challenge the dominant assumption that land must be productive or aesthetic to hold value
