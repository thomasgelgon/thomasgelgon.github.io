# Protecting Farmland in a Small Municipality

![Project overview image](../assets/images/project1-cover.png)

## Overview

In the context of France's Climate and Resilience Act, land use sobriety requires stronger protection of farmland, while local governments remain responsible for applying land law through the PLU (local planning document). Peri-urbanization, however, creates strong land development pressure. This case study of Lunel-Viel, Hérault, examines how the municipality co-creates planning regulations with the state to limit land take, reconciling environmental goals with residents' interests.

**Study Area:** Lunel-Viel, Hérault, France
**Duration:** 2 months
**Role:** Solo project
**Status:** Completed

---

## Methods & Tools

**Data Sources**

- Municipal planning documents (PLU, building permit records) — Lunel-Viel town hall
- INSEE demographic and migration data
- Semi-structured interviews (n=3) with municipal officials and elected representatives
- Academic literature (full bibliography in the dissertation)

**Processing Steps**

1. Literature review and theoretical framing
2. Data collection (PLU, building permit records, INSEE data)
3. Mapping of land artificialization and migration patterns
4. Typology of building permit applications in agricultural zones (by construction type and outcome)
5. Semi-structured interviews with municipal staff and elected officials
6. Cross-analysis and writing

**Tools Used**

| Tool | Purpose |

| Magrit | Thematic and statistical cartography |
| Adobe Suite | Layout and visual design of maps and figures |

## Key Findings

- Lunel-Viel proves more protective of farmland than existing literature on French peri-urban municipalities would suggest, favoring a proactive land-use policy over the permissive interpretations of planning law described elsewhere
- The municipality uses its PLU offensively in service of a resilient, agriculture-centered territorial project, well ahead of formal compliance with national land-take reduction targets (ZAN)
- Despite this proactive stance, some building permit applicants still exploit regulatory gray areas or resort to unauthorized construction, showing that local rule enforcement is always subject to case-by-case negotiation
