# Long-Term Vegetation Diversity Trends in North America: Evidence from Fossil Pollen

![Project overview image](../assets/images/project2-cover.png)

## Overview

Analysis of Simpson's diversity index from fossil pollen records at six North American lakes spanning the Holocene (11,000 years BP), exploring how vegetation diversity evolved across a wide latitudinal gradient and identifying the role of climate, latitude, and local conditions in shaping biodiversity trajectories.

**Study Area:** Six lake sites across North America (latitude range 35.8°N–67.7°N): Sweet Little Lake, Bell's Lake, Dead Spruce Lake, Harris Lake, Bison Lake, Stewart Bog
**Duration:** April 2025 (1 month)
**Role:** Solo project
**Status:** Completed

---

## Methods & Tools

**Data Sources**

- Fossil pollen count records — six North American lake sites (Routson et al., 2021 multiproxy database)

**Processing Steps**

1. Convert raw pollen counts to relative percentages
2. Calculate Simpson's diversity index through time for each site
3. Compare diversity trends, timing of peak diversity, and variability across sites
4. Analyze diversity-latitude relationships

**Tools Used**

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| R | Statistical analysis of pollen data and diversity index calculation |

---

## Key Findings

- Strong correlation between latitude and diversity during the early Holocene, with lower latitudes showing higher diversity — though Harris Lake (49.7°N, at the boreal-temperate transition) reached the highest diversity index (0.937)
- Two distinct groups emerged based on timing of peak diversity: an early-peaking group (mid-Holocene climatic optimum, ~4500–5300 BP) and a late-peaking group (peaking within the last few centuries)
- No single unified diversity pattern — trajectories depend on latitude, postglacial history, and local/regional specificities rather than climate alone
